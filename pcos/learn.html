Understanding Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)

What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal condition that affects how a woman's ovaries work. It is characterized by reproductive, endocrine, and metabolic abnormalities.

Despite its name, not all people with PCOS have cysts on their ovaries, and having cysts does not necessarily mean you have PCOS.

Common Symptoms

Symptoms usually become apparent during late teens or early twenties. They can include:

  • Irregular Periods: Menstrual cycles may be infrequent, prolonged, or unpredictable.
  • Excess Androgen: Elevated levels of male hormones may result in physical signs, such as excess facial and body hair (hirsutism), and occasionally severe acne.
  • Weight Changes: Unexplained weight gain or difficulty losing weight.
  • Skin Changes: Darkening of the skin (acanthosis nigricans), particularly along neck creases, in the groin, and underneath breasts.
  • Hair Thinning: Male-pattern baldness or thinning hair on the scalp.

Why Early Awareness Matters

Early diagnosis and treatment along with weight loss may reduce the risk of long-term complications such as type 2 diabetes and heart disease.

If you suspect you may have PCOS based on your symptoms or the results of our screening tool, we strongly recommend scheduling a visit with a healthcare provider, such as a gynecologist or endocrinologist.
